# Download Jolt, keep your rig on top

**Download the solver and build only the character layer, and prototype JoltPhysics.js first**, using the single-thread `wasm` build of `jolt-physics` 1.1.1. Jolt is the only free web engine in this research that exposes a ragdoll class, swing-twist limits and pose-driven motors to JavaScript, and it does so at about 606 KB brotli with no COOP/COEP headers. Rapier ranks second: it is often faster per step and has the largest ecosystem, but its typed JS API has no spherical joint limits or ragdoll class. PhysX ranks third as the specialist choice for stiff active ragdolls. The team should still write the bone mapping, joint data, drive logic, blending and caps, because no maintained three.js ragdoll library exists. `character-motion.js` stays as the source of target poses, and the hand-written ragdoll stays as the baseline until Jolt beats it on a real phone. **No researcher ran any engine on a phone, and no published phone benchmark with joints exists,** so this ranking rests on API inspection, measured file sizes and desktop tables. Neither the existing ragdoll nor `character-motion.js` was assessed. One outcome would overturn the whole recommendation: if the game only needs simple death ragdolls and the current code is already stable on phones, no engine is needed yet.

## Jolt wins on ragdoll API, not on raw speed

The deciding evidence is what each binding exposes. Jolt's JS interface includes `RagdollSettings`, `Ragdoll`, `DriveToPoseUsingMotors`, `DriveToPoseUsingKinematics`, `SwingTwistConstraintSettings` and `DisableParentChildCollisions` ([JoltJS.idl](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/blob/784c98c0c09e51c6d47700bc16fbbe8c211eb434/JoltJS.idl#L3912-L3992)). Its official [Powered Rig demo](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics.js/rig/powered_rig.html) drives a dynamic ragdoll with motors in the browser. The motor call takes a whole pose, and an overload also derives target velocities from the previous and current poses ([Ragdoll.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/master/Jolt/Physics/Ragdoll/Ragdoll.cpp)). That maps directly onto a procedural rig that already produces a pose every frame. The engine also has shipped pedigree: its README names Horizon Forbidden West and Death Stranding 2 ([Jolt README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/jrouwe/joltphysics@master/README.md)).

Rapier 0.21 has no ragdoll class, and its `SphericalImpulseJoint` has motor methods but no `setLimits` ([impulse_joint.ts](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/475caa3ff4646fd9a796b67d14116256f4962bc7/bindings/typescript/src.ts/dynamics/impulse_joint.ts#L424)). A 2024 request for those limits, citing ragdoll joints that "rotate infinitely", remains unanswered in the now-archived bindings repo ([rapier.js #290](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier.js/issues/290)). The WASM layer does have `jointSetLimits` ([impulse_joint.rs](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/475caa3ff4646fd9a796b67d14116256f4962bc7/bindings/typescript/src/dynamics/impulse_joint.rs#L185)), but that route is internal and untested, and it yields three per-axis ranges rather than a true swing cone. Active ragdolls would also need per-axis motor targets computed from each quaternion.

Speed points the other way, but the margin matters little at this scale. Two recent tables are closest to the use case, and neither is on a phone.

| ms per step, ragdolls awake | Rapier | Jolt | PhysX | cannon-es | Bearing (1 thread) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| [NexusMMO PR #543](https://github.com/SirHertzAlot/NexusMMOEngine/pull/543), 200 rigs of 11 parts, 2 substeps, headless Chromium | 32.9 | 41.1 | 15.7 | not tested | not tested |
| [Bearing README](https://github.com/getlowarc/bearing), 32 ragdolls, first 4 s, i7-1260P laptop (rival vendor, solver settings not matched) | 1.50 | 3.49 | not tested | 7.95 | 8.27 |
| Same Bearing test: widest joint gap at 8 s | 7.08 mm | 0.10 mm | not tested | 5.25 mm | 0.00 mm |

The marginal cost in the NexusMMO table works out to about 0.21 ms per awake ragdoll per tick for Jolt, 0.16 ms for Rapier and 0.08 ms for PhysX (my arithmetic from the 100 and 200 rig rows). So choosing Rapier over Jolt saves roughly 0.15 ms on desktop for three active ragdolls, while it costs a large amount of custom joint work. Jolt's single-thread npm build also lacks SIMD ([CMakeLists.txt](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/blob/784c98c0c09e51c6d47700bc16fbbe8c211eb434/CMakeLists.txt#L28)). A self-built SIMD version could narrow the gap, since the Godot team treats 1.5x to 2x as a representative WASM SIMD gain ([Godot blog](https://godotengine.org/article/upcoming-serious-web-performance-boost/)). The joint-gap row should not be quoted as an inherent engine difference, because more iterations could narrow it.

Three counter-signals deserve weight. A Godot benchmark from the Rapier integration's maintainer shows Jolt going unstable on a joint grid, though it had no ragdoll scene and is not independent ([godot.rapier.rs](https://godot.rapier.rs/blog/v0-35-0)). JoltPhysics.js diverged from native Jolt by 14 m over 10 s in a 60-body scene, so native results do not transfer exactly ([NexusMMO PR #543](https://github.com/SirHertzAlot/NexusMMOEngine/pull/543)). And Jolt demands manual `Jolt.destroy` calls, a real burden in a game that spawns many characters ([README](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/blob/784c98c0c09e51c6d47700bc16fbbe8c211eb434/README.md)).

## The ranking: three contenders, three niche options, and the rest ruled out

| Rank | Engine | Download, gzip | Ragdoll support in JS | Verdict |
|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Jolt 1.1.1 `wasm` | 881 KB (606 KB brotli) | Ragdoll class, swing-twist, pose motors | **Prototype first** |
| 2 | Rapier 0.21 `simd-compat` | 1,652 KB (1,177 KB brotli) | Per-axis motors; no typed spherical limits | Fallback; best ecosystem and determinism |
| 3 | PhysX `physx-js-webidl` 2.9.1 | 1,967 KB (1,365 KB brotli) | Reduced-coordinate articulations, PD drives, D6 | Spike only if Jolt's stiff drives fail |
| 4 | crashcat 0.0.5 | 46 KB, pure JS | Swing-twist, official ragdoll example; swing-twist motor API unconfirmed | Best pure-JS option; pre-1.0 |
| 5 | Havok 1.3.14 | 672 KB (513 KB brotli) | Per-axis constraints only; no ragdoll or cone type | Speed fallback; closed binary |
| 6 | Box3D via box3d.js 0.1.1 | 334 KB | Spherical joint with cone and twist limits, motors | Watch; JS API is young |
| 7 | cannon-es 0.20.0 | 23 KB | `ConeTwistConstraint` | Legacy; no release since 2022 |
| 8 | ammo.js (repo main) | 343 KB (258 KB brotli) | Cone-twist motor; no multibody in the IDL | Outclassed; wraps Bullet 2.82 |
| 9 | MuJoCo 3.17.0 | 2,756 KB (2,074 KB brotli) | Best actuators; compiled model | Wrong tool for a game |

Sizes for ranks 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and 9 were measured from npm tarballs or the ammo.js repo at maximum compression ([jolt-physics](https://unpkg.com/browse/jolt-physics@1.1.1/dist/), [rapier3d-simd-compat](https://unpkg.com/browse/@dimforge/rapier3d-simd-compat@0.21.0/dist/), [physx-js-webidl](https://www.npmjs.com/package/physx-js-webidl), [Havok](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@babylonjs/havok), [ammo.js](https://github.com/kripken/ammo.js/tree/main/builds), [MuJoCo](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@mujoco/mujoco)). Ranks 4, 6 and 7 use min+gzip figures from a different source, so compare them loosely ([bundle results](https://github.com/isaac-mason/js-physics-benchmarks/blob/main/bundle-sizes/results.json)). That same file lists Rapier 0.20 SIMD at only 762 KB against 1,145 KB measured for the 0.21 SIMD WASM alone. The [0.21.0 change enabling the `fem` soft-body solver](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/475caa3ff4646fd9a796b67d14116256f4962bc7/bindings/typescript/CHANGELOG.md) is a plausible cause, as is a custom benchmark build, but the research did not settle it.

PhysX has the only real technical claim to beat Jolt. NVIDIA names ragdolls as an articulation use case and claims "zero joint error by design" ([PhysX docs](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.6.1/docs/Articulations.html)). The same docs warn that stiff drives combined with contacts carry no guarantee, and that articulation joints are not breakable. PhysX's speed lead comes from one table, the bindings have a [sole maintainer](https://www.npmjs.com/package/physx-js-webidl), no public three.js articulation example was found, and its 64 MiB initial heap is a risk on older phones. The maintainer's [Kotlin ragdoll demo](https://fabmax.github.io/kool/kool-js/?demo=phys-ragdoll) shows it works in a browser.

crashcat is the surprise entrant: 46 KB, MIT, with a [live ragdoll example](https://crashcat.dev/examples/example-ragdoll.html). It is at version 0.0.5, with [breaking changes pending](https://github.com/isaac-mason/crashcat/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md), and its docs target "hundreds of dynamic bodies" on a desktop browser ([docs](https://crashcat.dev/docs/)). Bearing has the best self-reported joint figures but reached npm only on 2 November 2026, so it is one to watch ([Bearing](https://github.com/getlowarc/bearing)). Bounce documents no cone or swing-twist joint ([docs](https://codeberg.org/perplexdotgg/bounce/raw/branch/main/docs/documentation.md)). Tumble has shipped only canary builds ([repo](https://github.com/isaac-mason/tumble)). WebGPU engines such as [three-avbd](https://github.com/sbobyn/three-avbd) need `WebGPURenderer`, which rules them out for this WebGL target.

MuJoCo fails on workflow rather than quality: its web bindings are self-described WIP ([README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@mujoco/mujoco@3.17.0/README.md)), and mesh collision uses convex hulls ([docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)).

## r158 UMD fits with a module loader and hand-written glue

No engine integrates with r158 out of the box, so this constraint barely separates the candidates. The r158 tag ships only `AmmoPhysics.js` and `RapierPhysics.js`, as ES modules ([r158 physics](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r158/examples/jsm/physics)), and even current addons have no ragdoll helpers ([r187 physics](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r187/examples/jsm/physics)). Jolt's own demo shows the needed pattern: classic three.js scripts first, then a `<script type="module">` that imports Jolt, tested there at three.js r144 ([powered_rig.html](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/blob/784c98c0c09e51c6d47700bc16fbbe8c211eb434/Examples/rig/powered_rig.html#L14-L23)). Since physics engines only exchange numbers with the renderer, r158 should behave the same, but nobody tested it.

Self-host the `wasm` flavour and set `locateFile`, which gives streaming compilation and separate caching ([README](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/blob/784c98c0c09e51c6d47700bc16fbbe8c211eb434/README.md)). Rapier without a bundler must use its `-compat` packages, which inline base64 WASM because `init()` takes no URL ([init.d.ts](https://unpkg.com/@dimforge/rapier3d-compat@0.21.0/dist/init.d.ts)). Single-thread Jolt contains no `SharedArrayBuffer` use, so Netlify needs no special headers ([jolt-physics tarball](https://registry.npmjs.org/jolt-physics/-/jolt-physics-1.1.1.tgz)). Both Jolt and Rapier are permissively licensed, free and have no paid service ([Jolt](https://registry.npmjs.org/jolt-physics/latest), [Rapier](https://registry.npmjs.org/@dimforge%2frapier3d-compat/latest)).

Two gaps must be closed in the first hour. Jolt's current CMake defaults show a fixed 128 MB heap with growth off, while a 2025 maintainer comment described 64 MB, and the actual value in the npm build is unconfirmed ([CMakeLists.txt](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/blob/784c98c0c09e51c6d47700bc16fbbe8c211eb434/CMakeLists.txt#L34-L40), [issue #244](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/issues/244)). If 128 MB is right, Jolt's memory floor exceeds PhysX's and becomes the top phone risk. Current iPhone reliability is also unproven for every engine. A GitHub search found no Jolt iOS issues, which is weak evidence ([issues](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/issues?q=is%3Aissue%20iOS)), while Rapier's open iOS crash report dates from 2021 ([rapier.js #14](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier.js/issues/14)).

## Build the layer above the solver, in this order

A custom solver is the wrong investment. A well-made one can match engines on cost: the [carrona](https://github.com/agustinyarrus/carrona) game reports 40 XPBD ragdolls plus props in 9 ms. Its author's own list shows where the work goes, though: bodies thrown about 60 m, backward knees, explosions, and bone stretch reaching 116% before fixes. Those are the problems an engine has already solved. The hand-written ragdoll is defensible only while its contact needs stay simple.

What cannot be downloaded is the character layer. `SkeletonMapper` is absent from JoltJS.idl, and the ragdoll loader lives in the demos folder rather than the npm package ([JoltJS.idl](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/blob/784c98c0c09e51c6d47700bc16fbbe8c211eb434/JoltJS.idl), [ragdoll_loader.js](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js/blob/784c98c0c09e51c6d47700bc16fbbe8c211eb434/Examples/js/ragdoll_loader.js)). The team therefore writes the mapping from rig bones to ragdoll parts. Keep the bone map, capsule sizes and limits as engine-neutral data so a later swap stays cheap. The useful references are Jolt's `powered_rig`, [crashcat's example](https://github.com/isaac-mason/crashcat/blob/main/examples/src/example-ragdoll.ts) and [mattvb91's bone mapping](https://github.com/mattvb91/rapierjs-ragdoll). Nothing surveyed is a dependable library.

The technique order below is the research team's judgement, and the effort figures are estimates assuming a working engine integration. No published comparison of quality against effort exists.

| Order | Technique | Estimated effort | Key parameters |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clean passive ragdoll | 1–3 days | 12–13 capsules, adjacent collisions off, flattened mass, limits no tighter than 5–15°, fixed step |
| 2 | Inherit bone velocities, add impulse at the hit bone | Under 1 day | Keeps momentum continuous |
| 3 | Settle, classify face up or down, get up | 2–4 days | Pelvis orientation, 0.2–0.5 s blend, root aligned first |
| 4 | Phone budget controls | 1–2 days | Hard cap, no ragdoll-to-ragdoll collision, freeze on settle, canned fall past the cap |
| 5 | Upper-body hit reaction, legs procedural | 3–7 days | Strength dip, then ramp back over about 0.3–0.6 s |
| 6 | Whole-body soft drive with fallback | 1–2 weeks | Damping ratio 1, force caps, root assist, drop to free ragdoll when off target |
| 7 | Stumble using IK or impulses | 1–3 weeks | Blend heuristics rather than true balance |
| 8 | Physics-driven balance and locomotion | Weeks or more | Only if wobble is the game's design |
| 9 | Learned controllers | Months plus GPU time | Out of scope |

Several of these values have firm sources. Jolt's `Stabilize()` clamps parent-child mass ratios to 0.8–1.2 and raises parent inertia, so anatomical masses should not be used directly ([Ragdoll.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/master/Jolt/Physics/Ragdoll/Ragdoll.cpp)). Unity's stability guide gives the 5–15° minimum limit and warns that 10:1 mass ratios can jitter ([Unity](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/physics-section/physics-overview/ragdoll/ragdoll-stability.md)). Item 6 copies Jedi: Fallen Order, which constrains the hip to its target and switches to free ragdoll when the distance grows too large, so enemies slump against walls instead of clipping ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/leveraging-physical-animation-to-sell-force-powers-in-i-jedi-fallen-order-i-)). Naughty Dog's early physics animation looked poor because animation was physically unrealistic ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024594/Physics-Animation-in-Uncharted-4)), and procedural targets will likely hit the same wall. For blending out of ragdoll, inertialization needs only a few lines of decay code per bone ([Holden](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)), although no source tests it specifically on ragdoll recovery.

Learned controllers stay out for practical reasons. ASE needed over 10 billion samples and about 10 days on a V100 ([ASE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.01906)), and DeepMimic simulates at 1.2 kHz ([DeepMimic](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.02717)). A policy trained that way is unlikely to transfer to a 60 Hz WASM engine, and any change to the rig's proportions could force retraining.

## A phone test decides, and these results would change the pick

Start with two checks that take minutes: open the [Powered Rig demo](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics.js/rig/powered_rig.html) and run the [js-physics-benchmarks live page](https://isaac-mason.github.io/js-physics-benchmarks/) on a real iPhone and a mid-range Android phone. Then put a 13-body ragdoll driven by `character-motion.js` on a test deploy. Measure p95 step time, WASM compile time, tab memory, stretch after a hard throw, and pose tracking. Use `curl -I` to confirm Netlify's `.wasm` content type and compression, which were not verified ([Netlify blog](https://www.netlify.com/blog/2020/05/20/gain-instant-performance-boosts-as-brotli-comes-to-netlify-edge.md)).

A reasonable starting budget is 1–3 fully active and 4–8 passive ragdolls, which is the research team's estimate rather than a measured figure. As a cross-check, Jolt's single-thread time grew 4.3x under a 4x CPU throttle in [one desktop test](https://github.com/devmildfire/junk-physics-demo). If a phone behaves like that throttle, an awake ragdoll costs about 1 ms per tick at 0.21 ms on desktop, so three cost about 3 ms before motors and contacts. Treat that as a hypothesis for the test to confirm. I suggest 4 ms p95 at the target count as the pass line.

| If this turns out to be true | Then do this |
|---|---|
| Only simple death ragdolls are needed, and the current code holds up on phones with no stretch or explosions | Keep the hand-written ragdoll and apply techniques 2–4; add no engine |
| Jolt crashes on iPhone or its fixed heap is too large for target phones | Switch to Rapier |
| Cross-platform lockstep or rollback is required | Switch to Rapier's [`-deterministic` builds](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/475caa3ff4646fd9a796b67d14116256f4962bc7/bindings/typescript/README.md); Jolt [needs a custom build](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/#deterministic-simulation) |
| Rapier is at least 2x faster on phones at the target count, and `jointSetLimits` holds up in a test | Switch to Rapier and accept the custom joint work |
| Stiff active ragdolls in Jolt stay unstable after tuning, and active ragdolls are core to the game | Spike PhysX articulations on an iPhone |
| Download weight dominates on slow networks and crashcat's swing-twist motors exist, or only passive ragdolls are needed | Trial crashcat with a pinned version |
| Havok proves much faster on the real scene and per-axis limits look acceptable | Consider Havok; expect to write all ragdoll logic against a closed binary ([docs](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/physics/havokPlugin)) |

Multiplayer alone should not force the Rapier switch. The forum-level advice is to treat ragdolls as cosmetic and have the server decide only when they start ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/ragdoll-physic-replication/2692164)), and that removes the need for determinism.

## Conclusion

The usual web-physics comparison asks which engine steps fastest, and that is the wrong question here. At one to three active characters, the per-ragdoll differences are small compared with the cost of hand-building swing limits and pose drives, so the binding's API surface carries the decision. Popularity misleads as well: Rapier's 51.6 million monthly `-compat` downloads against Jolt's 37,000 ([npm](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-month/@dimforge/rapier3d-compat), [npm](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-month/jolt-physics)) say nothing about which is easier for a ragdoll.

The largest risk has moved from solver quality to phone memory and iOS behaviour, which no source measures, so a day of device testing is worth more than further reading. Since items 1–4 of the technique list deliver most of the visible gain and carry over to any engine, the team keeps most of that work even if the engine pick changes. The Jolt-specific calls should stay behind a thin interface.
